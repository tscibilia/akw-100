# AKW-100 · 40 Words

## The core rule

**AKW-40.1** A Keynote states **what** work is required and **what end
condition** the work must be left in.

It does not state **means and methods**: the contractor's sequence, technique,
equipment, labour, or safety.

It does not state **Extent**: where the work is or how much of it there is. The
Keynote Tag carries Extent. Holding Extent out of the words is what makes one
Keynote serve every location.

Naming a **termination point** or a **physical end state** is scope, not method:

```
CAP AND REMOVE BACK TO THE SOURCE PANEL, OR TO THE NEAREST JUNCTION BOX
```

That fixes where the work stops. How the conductor is pulled stays the
electrician's business.

## Vagueness

**AKW-40.2** Vagueness about Extent is permitted **only** when something else
decides it: a stated trigger, or a Reference Document.

| Phrase | Verdict | Decider |
|--------|---------|---------|
| `AS SCHEDULED` | Pass | The schedule |
| `PER PROPOSED RCP` | Pass | The RCP |
| `IN KIND` | Pass | The existing product |
| `TYPICAL, U.O.N.` | Pass | The notes that say otherwise |
| `AS NEEDED IF THE CONDITION UNCOVERS A VOID` | Pass | The stated trigger |
| `AS NEEDED` (bare) | **Fail** | Nothing |
| `AS REQUIRED` (bare) | **Fail** | Nothing |
| `WHERE INDICATED` (bare) | **Fail** | Nothing |

**AKW-40.3** Vagueness about the **end condition** is banned outright. No
decider can rescue it, because the end condition is what the Keynote exists to
fix.

Banned words and phrases:

```
MAKE GOOD        MINOR          ADEQUATE       PROPER
SUITABLE         REASONABLE     NEAT           WORKMANLIKE
AS APPROPRIATE   TO SUIT        GOOD CONDITION ACCEPTABLE
```

Replace the adjective with the condition:

- Defect: `MAKE MINOR REPAIRS TO THE HANDRAILS.`
- Fixed: `REPAIR TO A SOUND SURFACE, FREE OF FLAKING COATING, RUST, AND
  CORROSION, READY FOR COATING.`

The condition sets the limit of work. An adjective never does.

**AKW-40.4** `NOT LIMITED TO` widens scope in the contractor's favour, not
yours. It is banned unless widening is the deliberate intent, and then the
Keynote must also state the end condition that closes it.

## Direction words

**AKW-40.5** State a slope limit as `NOT STEEPER THAN 1:8` or `NOT FLATTER THAN
1:48`. `MIN.` and `MAX.` are banned next to a ratio.

A slope ratio inverts: a bigger second number means a gentler slope. `MINIMUM
SLOPE OF 1:8` reads to a contractor as permission to build 1:6, and they are
reading it correctly.

`MIN.` and `MAX.` remain legal with a plain dimension: `3 IN. MIN.`

## Word choice

**AKW-40.6** Use `DAMAGE`, never `DAMAGES`. In a contract document *damages*
means money awarded in a lawsuit.

**AKW-40.7** Write `AND`. The ampersand `&` is not a word and is easy to lose at
plot scale.

**AKW-40.8** A slash `/` means **or**. It is permitted only where either reading
is acceptable. Where both items are required, write `AND`.

- `ALTER/AFFECT`: either reading is acceptable. Pass.
- `POWER/LOW-VOLTAGE`: both are required. **Fail.** Write `POWER AND
  LOW-VOLTAGE`.

**AKW-40.9** Do not abbreviate a verb. `PREP` becomes `PREPARE`. Abbreviate
units only, per AKW-50.2.

**AKW-40.10** Name the thing precisely. `FLOORS` is not `FLOOR FINISHES`. `TRIM`
is not `CORNER GUARDS, WALL PROTECTION, AND BASE MOLDING`. A subcontractor
prices the cheapest defensible reading of the word you wrote.

**AKW-40.11** One term per concept, across the whole set. The canonical terms
are in `CONTEXT.md`. Do not vary a term for style.
